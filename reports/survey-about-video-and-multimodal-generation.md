# Video and Multimodal Generation: Architectures, Evaluation, Efficiency, and World Models

## TL;DR

- Video generation has converged on a small number of dominant designs: latent video diffusion with spatiotemporal factored attention, and — increasingly — a **diffusion-transformer (DiT)** trained on "spacetime latent patches," which scales markedly with compute and enabled minute-scale high-definition synthesis [1][2][3][4].
- A second, parallel lineage treats video as **discrete tokens** and models them with language-model-style autoregressive transformers (VideoGPT, MAGVIT, VideoPoet, Emu3), with the tokenizer — not the backbone — identified as the decisive ingredient [5][6][7][8][9].
- The single biggest engineering problem is cost: dense attention over 70k–150k tokens made a 5-second 720p clip take ~31 minutes on one H100, and the field now attacks this along four independent axes — latent compression, sparse/linear attention and caching, few-step distillation, and sequence/context parallelism [10][11][12][13][14][15].
- Evaluation has moved from single aggregate metrics (FVD, CLIPScore) — both documented as weakly aligned with human judgment — toward multi-dimensional suites (VBench, VBench-2.0, EvalCrafter), task-specific benchmarks, and Elo-based human-preference arenas [16][17][18][19][20][21][22].
- The 2024–2026 frontier reframes video generation as **interactive world modelling**: real-time playable worlds (Genie 3, GameNGen), persistent 3D worlds (Marble), and robot/embodied policy learning inside video world models — where physical plausibility and long-horizon memory remain the open failures [23][24][25][26][27][28][29].

## Background

"Video and multimodal generation" refers to generative models that synthesise video, and increasingly that jointly model video together with text, images, audio, actions, or 3D structure. It matters now for three reasons. First, the architecture question has effectively been settled and industrialised: the Sora technical report showed that a **diffusion transformer operating on spacetime latent patches** scales as a video model, with quality improving "markedly as training compute increases" and world-simulator-like behaviours emerging "purely as phenomena of scale" [1]. Second, video is the modality where the cost and evaluation problems are sharpest — the sequence lengths involved (tens of thousands of tokens per clip) are far beyond those of image generation, which forces genuinely new systems work [10][15]. Third, the output of these models is increasingly used as a *substrate* — a simulator, a policy, or an environment — rather than as a piece of media, which changes what "good" means [23][26][27].

The technical arc runs from pre-diffusion video modelling, through the first video diffusion models [2], cascaded text-to-video systems [3], latent video diffusion [30], token-based autoregressive generators [5][6][8], and finally diffusion transformers [1][31] and causal/autoregressive diffusion variants that can stream [32][33][34]. Several recent surveys map this evolution [4][35][15].

## Theme 1 — Architectures and generative paradigms: from space-time U-Nets to diffusion transformers

**Era 1: the video diffusion starting point.** Video Diffusion Models (VDM) was the first video model built on diffusion, described as a natural extension of the standard image diffusion architecture: a **factorized space-time 3D U-Net** trained jointly on images and video, with a gradient-based conditional sampling method for spatial and temporal extension and block-autoregressive long video [2]. Imagen Video then made text-conditional generation concrete with a **cascade of seven sub-models** (a frozen T5 text encoder, a base video model, three spatial super-resolution models, three temporal super-resolution models) totalling on the order of 11.6B parameters (a frozen T5-XXL encoder of about 4.6B plus a base video model of about 5.6B plus the super-resolution stages) and producing 128 frames at 1280×768 and 24 fps [3]. Notably, Imagen Video already argued for **block generation over frame-autoregression**, because denoising multiple frames simultaneously "is important to capture the temporal coherence of the generated video," and it introduced space-time-separable temporal attention plus progressive distillation for fast sampling [3].

**Era 2: latent video diffusion and the pixel/latent trade-off.** Video LDM (Align your Latents) moved video diffusion into a pretrained image latent space by adding a temporal dimension to the image LDM and fine-tuning on encoded video, then temporally aligning diffusion upsamplers into video super-resolution models, reaching up to 1280×2048 for text-to-video [30]. A 2025 survey frames the trade-off crisply: pixel-space models (Imagen Video, Make-A-Video, PYoCo) align motion well with text but demand expensive compute, while latent models (Video LDM, MagicVideo) are far more resource-efficient at the risk of losing semantic detail through compression [4]. Emu Video took a third route, factorizing text-to-video into **image-then-video**: generate an image from the prompt, then generate a video conditioned on both the prompt and that image, avoiding a deep cascade entirely and reporting human-eval wins of 81% / 90% / 96% against Imagen Video, PYoCo and Make-A-Video respectively [36].

**Era 3: token-based, LLM-style video generation.** A parallel lineage discretises video and applies language modelling. VideoGPT used a VQ-VAE with 3D convolutions and axial self-attention to learn downsampled discrete latents, then a simple GPT-like autoregressive model with spatio-temporal position encodings [5]. MAGVIT introduced a 3D video tokenizer with masked (BERT-style) token prediction, setting best-published FVD on Kinetics-600 while being two orders of magnitude faster than diffusion models and ~60× faster than autoregressive baselines at inference [6]. MAGVIT-v2 made the strongest claim of this line — "Language Model Beats Diffusion — Tokenizer is Key to Visual Generation" — arguing that a sufficiently good video tokenizer lets generic LLMs outperform diffusion for image and video generation [7]. VideoPoet scaled this to a decoder-only transformer processing images, videos, text and audio with LLM-style pretraining, achieving zero-shot video generation with high-fidelity motion [8]. Emu3 later argued the extreme version, "Next-Token Prediction is All You Need," training purely with next-token prediction and outperforming diffusion and compositional approaches on both generation and perception, while Chameleon demonstrated mixed-modal early-fusion training over interleaved tokens [9][37].

**Era 4: diffusion transformers and causal/streaming generation.** Sora generalised the DiT paradigm to video, compressing video into a latent and decomposing it into **spacetime patches that act as transformer tokens**, enabling variable duration, resolution and aspect ratio, and up to a minute of HD video; a companion review reverse-engineers it as a three-part system (a time-space compressor, a ViT-style denoiser, and CLIP-like conditioning with LLM-augmented instructions), and notes the report omits implementation details [1][31]. The most consequential recent shift is making these bidirectional DiTs **causal and streaming**: CausVid adapts a pretrained bidirectional DiT into a causal autoregressive transformer and extends distribution matching distillation to compress a 50-step model into a 4-step generator using ODE-trajectory initialisation and asymmetric distillation, reaching 84.27 on VBench-Long at 9.4 FPS streaming on a single GPU, versus 219 s for a 128-frame bidirectional baseline [32][33]. This "diffusion forcing" family — models that stay structurally close to diffusion but introduce temporal causality through autoregressive conditioning — is now a major sub-field, alongside consistency-model approaches such as VideoLCM that target minimal sampling steps [35][38][34]. Modern open foundation models are diffusion transformers with 3D VAEs: HunyuanVideo (13B+), HunyuanVideo 1.5 (an 8.3B DiT with a 3D causal VAE and selective/sliding tile attention), LTX-Video (a real-time video latent diffusion model), and Movie Gen, a media foundation suite that generates video with synchronised audio [39][14][40][41].

**Conditioning.** Text conditioning across these systems typically uses a frozen large text encoder (T5 or a CLIP-like encoder) feeding cross-attention, plus classifier-free guidance — both of which transfer from the image setting to video [3] — and, in Sora's case, LLM-augmented user instructions [31]. Scale trends are explicit: joint image+video training, efficient video VAEs, and large tokenizer compression ratios are treated as first-class design variables [1][39][42].

## Theme 2 — Evaluation: from FVD to multi-dimensional and intrinsic-faithfulness benchmarks

**Aggregate metrics are demonstrably weak.** FVD [16] has been the default video metric for years, but "On the Content Bias in Fréchet Video Distance" (CVPR 2024) shows FVD on I3D features is strongly biased toward per-frame quality over motion: large temporal corruptions barely move the score, and sampling motionless "frozen" videos can halve it [17]. CLIPScore — widely used for text-video alignment — was reported by the EvalCrafter study to correlate weakly with human judgement on the alignment aspect, well below BLIP-BLEU, which the authors propose as a better proxy [21]. JEDi (JEPA embedding distance) was proposed as a replacement that needs only 16% of the samples to stabilise and improves alignment with human evaluation by 34% on average [18].

**The response has been multi-dimensional suites.** VBench decomposes video quality into **16 disentangled dimensions** (subject consistency, background consistency, temporal flickering, motion smoothness, dynamic degree, aesthetic and imaging quality, object class, multiple objects, human action, colour, spatial relationship, scene, temporal style, appearance style, overall consistency), each with tailored prompts and methods, and reports high correlation with human preference rankings [19]. VBench-2.0 shifts from superficial to **intrinsic faithfulness** across five categories — Human Fidelity, Controllability, Creativity, Physics, Commonsense — broken into 18 fine-grained dimensions, combining generalist VLM/LLM judges with specialist anomaly detectors and safeguards against judge hallucination [20]. EvalCrafter uses 700 prompts and 17 objective metrics and explicitly fits coefficients to subjective user opinion [21]. Specialised benchmarks fill compositional gaps: T2V-CompBench targets consistent/dynamic attributes, spatial relationships, motion-action binding, object interactions and generative numeracy [22], and audio-visual evaluation has grown its own family, reflected in recent daily-listed work such as OmniCapBench for deep-structured audio-visual captioning [43].

**Human preference is now measured directly.** Blind pairwise voting arenas rank text-to-video models by Elo, complementing automated suites — a practical response to the fact that no single automated metric is trusted [21][19][20].

## Theme 3 — Controllability, multimodal joint generation, and alignment

**Camera and motion control is the most mature controllability axis.** CameraCtrl adds precise camera-pose control to text-to-video models [44]; MotionCtrl is a unified controller that independently drives camera and object motion [45]; Direct-a-Video decouples camera movement from object motion by modulating spatial and temporal cross-attention [35]. Structural conditioning follows the ControlNet pattern: SparseCtrl injects sparse structure signals (depth, pose, sketch at a few frames) into text-to-video models while reducing computation and supporting multiple modalities, and Ctrl-Adapter adapts pretrained ControlNets to arbitrary diffusion backbones for both image and video [46].

**Personalisation and identity preservation.** Multi-subject open-set personalisation from reference images — without per-subject test-time optimisation — is now demonstrated inside a diffusion transformer with cross-attention-based conditioning [47]. This is one of the clearest examples of video generation inheriting and then extending an image-generation capability.

**Joint audio–video and unified multimodal generation.** Video+audio co-generation has consolidated: MMAudio tames multimodal joint training for high-quality, semantically aligned and visually synchronised video-to-audio synthesis [48], and Movie Gen generates video with synchronised audio natively [41]. Speech-driven talking-head synthesis constitutes its own sub-field, spanning NeRF and Gaussian-splatting methods as well as DiT pipelines, all prioritising audio-lip synchronisation [49]. On the unified side, Emu3 and Chameleon represent single-objective, token-based any-to-any models [9][37].

**Alignment, prompting and provenance.** Reward-model-based alignment for visual generation is being scaled (RewardDance, which targets reward hacking), and RLHF/DPO-style preference optimisation has been adapted to video safety and helpfulness [50]. Prompt rewriting and expansion act as a pre-processing alignment layer, and provenance work includes in-generation video watermarking as a defence against deepfake misuse [50][43]. Two structural caveats are worth stating: safety alignment for video lags image alignment, and dataset licensing for video training corpora remains comparatively under-documented in the technical literature [21][50].

## Theme 4 — Efficiency, systems, and scaling

**The cost baseline.** Video DiTs run tens of full forward passes over 70k–150k-token sequences per clip. Producing a 5-second 720p clip with Wan2.1-14B takes roughly 31 minutes on one H100, and NVIDIA reports that a single 5-second 720p clip still "takes over five minutes" on Wan 2.2 T2V-A14B class models [10]. Dense attention is quadratic in that sequence length, so four levers are used in combination: latent compression, efficient attention, caching, and distillation [15].

**Latent compression sets the token budget.** HunyuanVideo 1.5 uses a 3D causal VAE with 16× spatial and 4× temporal compression and 32 latent channels, with an 8.3B DiT, and — using offloading and VAE tiling — runs the whole 720p 121-frame pipeline in 13.6 GB peak memory on a single RTX 4090 [14]. Step-Video-T2V pushes compression to 16×16 spatial and 8× temporal with a 30B full-attention DiT generating up to 204 frames [42]. Open-Sora 1.2 stacks an 8×8 spatial image VAE with a Magvit-v2-style 3D VAE adding 4× temporal compression [51].

**Efficient attention and caching.** Sparse VideoGen classifies attention heads into spatial and temporal types with online profiling and achieves 2.28×/2.33× end-to-end speedups on CogVideoX-v1.5 and HunyuanVideo while preserving quality [11]; HunyuanVideo 1.5's selective and sliding tile attention is parameter-free and integrated during distillation [14]. Caching is entirely training-free: AdaCache caches residual computations with a content-adaptive schedule and a motion-regularisation term, reporting up to 4.7× speedup on Open-Sora at 720p/2s [12]. A dedicated survey taxonomises diffusion caching as an evolution from static reuse to dynamic prediction [15], and recent work extends the idea to unified caching-plus-pruning and to sparse attention routing for long video [52][53][54].

**Distillation and few-step sampling.** Distribution matching distillation is the reference technique: DMD2 removes the regression loss, adds a GAN loss and two time-scale updates, achieving one-step generation with a 500× inference-cost reduction [13]. Applied to video, this produced the causal/streaming results discussed above — a 4-step causal generator at 9.4 FPS [32][33] — and subsequent work pushes further: frame-wise 2-step distillation with reduced first-frame latency and Causal Forcing-style autoregressive teacher initialisation [34][55], plus real-time interactive motion control described as reaching up to 29 FPS on a single GPU [56]. Recent daily-listed papers concentrate heavily on this axis, including sparse-attention gap analysis and mixture-of-groups attention for long video [54][57].

**Parallelism, serving and long-context memory.** HunyuanVideo trains with 5D parallelism (tensor, sequence, context parallelism with Ring Attention, plus data parallelism and cache sharding) and notes attention becomes the training bottleneck as sequence length grows [39]. Open-Sora uses Ulysses-style sequence parallelism applied to the spatial block, cutting 720p/16s inference from 547.97 s on one GPU to 244.38 s on two [51]. At serving scale, TensorRT-LLM's VisualGen unifies classifier-free-guidance parallelism, Ulysses, 2D context parallelism and parallel VAE, reaching ~49× speedup on the denoise loop at 72 GPUs with ~67% scaling efficiency versus ~37% for an equivalent Ring configuration [10]. For autoregressive video, KV-cache memory is a first-class target: VideoMLA reduces per-token KV memory by 92.7% [58].

**Data and scaling laws.** Data curation and captioning at scale underpin training quality: Panda-70M curates 3.8M high-resolution videos into 70M clips with cross-modality teacher captions, improving captioning, retrieval and text-to-video generation [59], and Stable Video Diffusion demonstrated the value of a systematic data-curation process when scaling latent video diffusion [60]. HunyuanVideo explicitly fits text-to-video scaling laws and, balancing training and inference cost, selects a 13B model size [39]. The honest caveat is that unlike language modelling, no consensus video scaling law has emerged — the published fits are model-family-specific [39][15].

## Theme 5 — World models, embodied generation, and applications

**Interactive, playable world generation.** Google DeepMind's Genie line is the anchor: Genie 2 is an autoregressive latent diffusion model producing action-controllable playable worlds from a single image, consistent for up to about a minute [61]. Genie 3 generates interactive worlds from a text prompt, navigable in real time at 24 FPS and 720p, remaining largely consistent for several minutes with visual memory reaching back about a minute, and supports promptable events; it shipped initially as a limited research preview [23]. The neural-game-engine proof point is GameNGen, which simulates DOOM at over 20 FPS on a single TPU with PSNR 29.4, where human raters were only slightly better than chance at distinguishing generated clips — although its effective history context is short, reported at only a few seconds, which limits long-horizon interaction [24]. An open research stream attacks the same two bottlenecks of real-time streaming and long-horizon memory, e.g. Matrix-Game 2.0/3.0 [62][63] and WorldCrafter's implicit 3D-aware memory [64]. World Labs' Marble takes a different stance, producing *persistent* downloadable 3D worlds (Gaussian splats, meshes, or videos) from text, images, video or coarse 3D layouts rather than generating on the fly, arguing that persistence reduces morphing [25].

**Model-based RL and the Dreamer tradition.** DreamerV3 learns a world model and improves behaviour by imagining futures, outperforming specialised methods across 150+ diverse tasks with a single fixed hyperparameter configuration, and was the first algorithm to collect diamonds in Minecraft from scratch without human data or curricula [65][66]. Dreamer 4 extends this to behaviour learned entirely by imagination inside a scalable world model from offline data only [67].

**Embodied AI and robotics.** A rapidly growing cluster treats generated video as a simulator or supervision signal: video generators used directly as robot policies [26], scalable policy evaluation with video world models [27], and action-faithful, goal-directed embodied world models such as DreamTrue and WorldGuide [68][69]. NVIDIA's Cosmos world foundation models are the industry counterpart, released openly for physical AI and adopted for synthetic data generation in robotics [70].

**Emerging applications.** Beyond games and robotics, retrieved sources cover instruction-based video editing with large synthetic datasets [71], 4D and dynamic 3D Gaussian-splatting generation from video [72], virtual try-on and e-commerce video via video diffusion [73], and autonomous-driving world models used as synthetic data generators [70]. The industry landscape is now split between closed hosted systems and open-weight releases, with licensing varying substantially between tiers — the Wan series and HunyuanVideo being notable open-weight options, and "open weight" being explicitly distinct from "open source" [74][75][39].

**Open problems.** Physical plausibility is the headline failure. A survey of physics cognition in video generation characterises the field's core deficiency as "visual realism but physical absurdity" — generated content frequently violates fundamental physical laws — and proposes a progression from visual mimicry toward physical comprehension [29]. WorldModelBench quantifies this: the top-performing model correctly completed the specified task in only about 61% of generated videos, the benchmark explicitly penalises violations of the mass-conservation law, and its physics score correlates only weakly (around 0.28) with VBench-based measures [28]. Long-horizon consistency and memory remain unsolved even at the frontier: the systems reported to hold minutes of coherence do so with explicit memory mechanisms, and evaluation of exactly that property is immature [64][23][28]. Evaluation validity itself is disputed — automated metrics are known to be poorly aligned, benchmark contamination is hard to rule out, and physics-aware evaluation ladders have only recently been proposed [17][18][28]. Finally, cost remains a practical barrier: although distillation and parallelism have produced order-of-magnitude gains [13][10], figures like "$13.6 GB peak memory on a single 4090" are the exception rather than the norm [14], and video cost per second of output remains far above image generation [15][10].

## Trends and open problems

**Trends over the last two years.** (1) *Architectural convergence:* the field moved from cascaded pixel-space diffusion U-Nets [3] to single-stage latent diffusion transformers over spacetime patches [1][4], with token-based LLM-style backbones as the principal alternative [7][8][9]. (2) *Causality and streaming:* bidirectional denoising is being replaced or distilled into causal, autoregressive, KV-cached generators capable of real-time interaction [32][33][34][56]. (3) *Evaluation reform:* the field abandoned reliance on FVD and CLIPScore after documented misalignment [17][18][21] and built multi-dimensional, intrinsic-faithfulness and compositional suites instead [19][20][22]. (4) *Reframing as world models:* video generation is increasingly presented and benchmarked as a simulator or environment rather than as media [23][61][24][28]. (5) *Efficiency as a first-class research area:* by 2026 the daily-listed literature is dominated by sparse attention, caching, and few-step distillation variants rather than new base architectures [54][57][55][15].

**Unsolved and disputed.** Physics and causality remain unsolved: strong visual quality coexists with systematic violations of conservation laws and object permanence, and the benchmark evidence (low task-completion rates, explicit mass-conservation violations, weak correlation with appearance-centric metrics such as VBench) suggests this is a capability gap, not a metric artefact [28][29]. Long-horizon coherence and memory are unsolved and only partially measured [64][23][28]. Evaluation validity is disputed — specifically, whether VLM/LLM judges can be trusted for faithfulness [20], whether reported FVD improvements reflect real motion quality [17], and whether automatic metrics can be replaced at all by sample-efficient learned distances [18]. Scaling behaviour is unsettled: Sora reports marked improvement with compute [1] and HunyuanVideo fits a family-specific scaling law to choose 13B [39], but there is no broadly validated video scaling law comparable to those in language modelling [15]. On the systems side, the economics of long-context autoregressive video (KV-cache growth, first-frame latency, per-request cost) remain an active target rather than a solved problem [58][10]. Finally, safety, provenance and licensing for video are comparatively immature relative to the quality of the generated output [50][21].
